0. Master Roadmap: Where CivPro II Begins

KWENTO: Tapos na si plaintiff mag-present at mag-formal offer ng evidence, at nag-rest na siya. Dito nagsisimula ang practical focus ng CivPro II: puwede bang tapusin ang kaso without completing a full trial, paano i-challenge ang judgment, kailan final na, paano i-execute, at anong temporary remedies ang available habang pending ang main action?
Visual overview: from Plaintiff Rests, to judgment-stage devices, appeals, finality, exceptional remedies, and Rule 39 execution.

Three questions you should ask in almost every remedial-law problem

  1. What stage is the case in? Before judgment, after judgment but before finality, or after finality?
  2. What court rendered the assailed judgment/order and in what jurisdiction? MTC, RTC original, RTC appellate, quasi-judicial agency, CA, COMELEC/COA?
  3. What exactly is being attacked? Error of fact/law, jurisdictional error/grave abuse, or a final judgment allegedly void or procured through extrinsic fraud?
Core doctrine
Certiorari under Rule 65 is not a substitute for a lost appeal. Always identify the proper ordinary remedy first.

1. Rule 33 — Demurrer to Evidence

KWENTO: Nakapag-present na si plaintiff at nag-rest. Sabi ni defendant: “Kahit tanggapin natin ang evidence niya as presented, kulang pa rin para magkaroon siya ng right to relief.” That is the basic logic of a civil demurrer to evidence.

Rule

Ground: upon the facts and the law, the plaintiff has shown no right to relief. In practical terms, the evidence is insufficient to establish the plaintiff’s cause.
PointRule 33
Who files?Defendant.
When?After plaintiff has completed presentation of evidence and rested.
What does court test?Legal and evidentiary sufficiency of plaintiff’s case.
If denied?Defendant proceeds to present evidence.
If granted?Case is dismissed on the merits; plaintiff may appeal the judgment.
If grant is reversed on appeal?Defendant is deemed to have waived the right to present evidence; appellate court resolves on the existing record as the Rules direct.
Verified Correction / Clarification: Under the 2019 Amendments, the order denying a demurrer to evidence is not subject to appeal or petition for certiorari, prohibition, or mandamus before judgment. Do not use the older shortcut “interlocutory = Rule 65 available” here; the amended text expressly restricts these attacks before judgment.

Civil vs Criminal Demurrer

PointCivil — Rule 33Criminal — Rule 119, Sec. 23
Filed afterPlaintiff restsProsecution rests
Leave of courtNot the controlling distinctionCritical; filing without leave has waiver consequences if denied
Effect of grantDismissal appealable by plaintiffAcquittal generally protected by double jeopardy; prosecution’s recourse is exceptionally limited
Effect of reversalDefendant loses right to present evidenceDifferent due-process/double-jeopardy framework
Recitation one-liner

Rule 33 tests whether, after the plaintiff rests, the evidence is legally sufficient to establish a right to relief.

2. Rule 34 — Judgment on the Pleadings

KWENTO: May answer nga si defendant, pero wala naman talagang issue: either inamin niya ang material allegations or ang denial niya is legally ineffective. So bakit pa mag-trial?
When proper: the answer fails to tender an issue or otherwise admits the material allegations of the adverse party’s pleading.

What the court looks at

Judgment on the pleadings is fundamentally based on the pleadings themselves. The defect is not lack of evidence; it is the absence of a real issue in the answer.

ExampleResult
Defendant expressly admits material allegationsJOP may be proper.
Defendant makes a defective denial of an actionable document when a specific denial under oath is requiredThe matter may be deemed admitted; JOP may become proper depending on the pleadings.
Answer raises payment, novation, fraud, or another genuine factual defenseJOP generally improper because an issue is tendered.
Mandatory proof despite admissions
In actions for declaration of nullity or annulment of marriage or for legal separation, the material facts alleged in the complaint must still be proved.
Verified 2019 rule: The court may, motu proprio or on motion, render judgment on the pleadings when the answer fails to tender an issue or admits the material allegations. Any action of the court on a motion for judgment on the pleadings is expressly not subject to appeal or petitions for certiorari, prohibition, or mandamus.

3. Rule 35 — Summary Judgment

KWENTO: Sa pleadings mukhang may issue. Pero pag tiningnan ang affidavits, admissions, depositions, at documents, fake/sham lang pala ang issue. No genuine issue of material fact = no reason for a full trial.
Two requirements: (1) no genuine issue as to any material fact; and (2) movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.

JOP vs Summary Judgment

Judgment on PleadingsSummary Judgment
Apparent issue in answer?No real issue tenderedIssue appears in pleadings, but evidence shows it is not genuine
Materials consideredPleadingsPleadings + affidavits + depositions + admissions + other proper materials
Core testNo issueNo genuine issue of material fact
Material fact
A fact is material if it can affect the outcome under the governing substantive law. A factual disagreement over an irrelevant detail does not bar summary judgment.
Verified 2019 rule: The adverse party may file a comment and opposing affidavits/depositions/admissions within a non-extendible five (5) calendar days from receipt of the motion. A hearing is not automatic; the court may order one. Any action of the court on the motion is expressly not subject to appeal or petitions for certiorari, prohibition, or mandamus.

4. Rule 36 — Judgments, Final Orders & Entry

Requisites of a judgment/final order on the merits

  • In writing.
  • Personally and directly prepared by the judge.
  • Clearly and distinctly states the facts and the law on which it is based.
  • Signed by the judge.
  • Filed with the clerk of court.

Final for appeal vs final for execution

Sense of “final”Meaning
Final order / judgment for purposes of appealDisposes of the case or a distinct appealable matter; nothing substantial remains regarding the merits involved in that adjudication.
Final and executoryNo longer subject to ordinary appeal because the period lapsed, the right was waived/abandoned, or appellate review was exhausted.
Immutability
Once final and executory, a judgment generally becomes immutable and unalterable. Recognized exceptions are narrow, such as clerical corrections, nunc pro tunc entries, void judgments, and exceptional supervening circumstances affecting execution.

5. Rule 37 — Motion for New Trial / Reconsideration

Motion for New TrialMotion for Reconsideration
Basic ideaReopen proceedings because fairness or newly discovered evidence warrants a new trial.Ask the same court to reconsider the judgment based on errors shown by the record.
GroundsFAME + newly discovered evidenceDamages excessive; evidence insufficient; decision/final order contrary to law
PeriodWithin period to appealWithin period to appeal
Effect if timelyInterrupts finality and triggers the fresh-period doctrine where applicableSame basic effect where doctrine applies

Newly discovered evidence — quick test

  1. Discovered after trial.
  2. Could not have been discovered and produced at trial despite reasonable diligence.
  3. Material, not merely cumulative/corroborative/impeaching.
  4. Probably changes the result.
Exam trap
An order denying MNT/MR is generally not separately appealed. The appeal is from the underlying judgment/final order, raising the denial as an assigned error where proper.

6. Appeals & Review — Rules 40, 41, 42, 43, 45 and 64

KWENTO: Hindi puwedeng “appeal lang.” Kailangan alam mo: saan galing ang decision, original or appellate jurisdiction ba, anong issues ang iaakyat, at saang court pupunta.
RuleFrom → ToModeTypical scopeBasic period
40MTC → RTCNotice of appealFact, law, mixed15 days; 30 if record on appeal required
41RTC original → CAOrdinary appeal / notice of appealFact, law, mixed15 days; 30 if record on appeal required
42RTC appellate → CAPetition for reviewFact, law, mixed15 days, extension subject to Rule

Important correction: Do not memorize Rule 64 as a “15-day appeal.” Rule 64, Sec. 3 provides a 30-day period from notice of judgment/final order/resolution. A timely MR/MNT interrupts the period, but after denial only the remaining period runs, never less than five days.

Rule 40 — MTC to RTC

  • Notice of appeal filed with the MTC.
  • RTC reviews as appellate court.
  • Appellant’s memorandum is crucial after notice that the record is complete.
  • When MTC dismissed for lack of jurisdiction but RTC actually has original jurisdiction, Rule 40 contains special treatment allowing the RTC to proceed according to the Rule rather than reflexively dismiss.

Rule 41 — RTC original to CA

  • Ordinary appeal.
  • Notice of appeal filed in the RTC.
  • Record on appeal applies in special proceedings and cases allowing multiple/separate appeals.
  • Residual jurisdiction remains in the trial court before transmittal of the records for specified matters.

Rule 42 — RTC appellate to CA

Discretionary petition for review. It is not the same as a Rule 41 ordinary appeal.

Rule 43 — quasi-judicial agencies to CA

Used to review errors of fact, law, or mixed fact and law of covered quasi-judicial agencies. Filing ordinarily does not stay the challenged agency decision unless the CA orders otherwise.

Rule 45 — appeal by certiorari

Rule 45 is an appeal, despite the phrase “by certiorari.” It generally addresses questions of law. Factual review is exceptional.

Rule 45 vs Rule 65

Rule 45Rule 65
NatureAppealOriginal special civil action
Main errorError of judgment / lawLack/excess of jurisdiction or grave abuse amounting thereto
Typical period15 days60 days, subject to Rule 65
MR prerequisiteNot universally indispensableGenerally required unless recognized exception applies
Effect on judgmentGoverned by rules on appeal/finalityDoes not by itself stay execution
Never say
“Rule 65 is the backup when the appeal period was missed.” That is exactly the trap. Rule 65 is not a substitute for appeal.

7. Rule 38 — Petition for Relief from Judgment, Order, or Proceeding

KWENTO: Final na ang judgment, pero hindi dahil pinabayaan mo lang. Na-deprive ka ng fair chance to protect yourself because of FAME: Fraud, Accident, Mistake, or Excusable Negligence.

Nature

  • Extraordinary and equitable.
  • Available only to a party to the original proceeding.
  • Filed in the same court and same case.
  • Not a substitute for appeal lost through ordinary negligence.

Two situations

  1. Judgment/final order/proceeding was entered or taken against a party through FAME → ask that it be set aside.
  2. Party was prevented by FAME from taking an appeal → ask that the appeal be given due course.

Double period

Both must be satisfied: within 60 days from knowledge of the judgment/final order/proceeding AND not more than 6 months from entry/taking thereof.

Contents

Verified petition accompanied by affidavits showing the FAME relied upon and the facts constituting the petitioner’s good and substantial cause of action or defense.

Fraud

Extrinsic fraudIntrinsic fraud
Prevents a party from fairly presenting the case or defense — e.g., deceptive conduct that keeps the party away from court.Fraud occurring in the trial itself, such as perjured testimony or falsified evidence that could have been contested during the proceedings.
Can support Rule 38 when requisites are met.Ordinarily not enough for Rule 38.

8. Rule 47 — Annulment of Judgments or Final Orders and Resolutions

KWENTO: Final na ang RTC judgment, ordinary remedies are no longer available through no fault attributable to the petitioner, and the attack is based on the narrow grounds allowed by Rule 47.

Nature and venue

  • Original action in the Court of Appeals.
  • Targets final judgments/orders/resolutions of the RTC in civil actions when ordinary remedies are no longer available through no fault of the petitioner.

Grounds

GroundPeriodKey point
Extrinsic fraudWithin 4 years from discoveryNot available if the fraud was or could have been used as ground for new trial or petition for relief.
Lack of jurisdictionBefore barred by laches or estoppelJurisdictional nullity, not mere error of judgment.

Rule 38 vs Rule 47

Rule 38Rule 47
Where filedSame court, same caseCA, original action
Main groundsFAMEExtrinsic fraud or lack of jurisdiction
Period60 days from knowledge + 6 months from entry/taking4 years from discovery for extrinsic fraud; jurisdiction subject to laches/estoppel
CharacterContinuation in the same proceedingIndependent original action

9. Rule 39 — Execution, Satisfaction & Effect of Judgments

KWENTO: Nanalo ka nga, pero kung hindi mo ma-enforce, papel lang ang victory. Rule 39 converts the adjudication into actual compliance.

A. Execution as a matter of right

When a judgment or final order is final and executory, execution ordinarily issues as a matter of right on motion. The court’s duty is ministerial once the requisites are present.

Verified correction to the lecture note on “notice of hearing / scrap of paper”: Under the 2019 Rule 15, a motion for issuance of a writ of execution is listed as a non-litigious motion and is not set for hearing. By contrast, a motion for execution pending appeal is a litigious motion; opposition is due within five calendar days, and a hearing is discretionary with the court. The old blanket “no notice of hearing = scrap of paper” formulation should not be used as the current general rule for an ordinary motion for writ of execution.

B. Discretionary execution / execution pending appeal

  • Exception to the rule that execution waits for finality.
  • Requires motion, notice to adverse party, hearing, and good reasons stated in a special order.
  • Good reasons must be superior circumstances demanding immediate execution; mere winning is not enough.
  • May be stayed by sufficient supersedeas bond when allowed, subject to the Rule and the court’s determination.

C. Five years / ten years

ENTRY OF FINAL JUDGMENT │ ├─ Within 5 years → execution by MOTION │ └─ After 5 years but before barred by prescription → INDEPENDENT ACTION to revive │ └─ Revived judgment: enforceable by motion within 5 years from its entry, then again by action before prescription bars it.

D. Writ must conform to judgment

The writ is only the instrument of enforcement. It cannot enlarge, alter, or vary the dispositive portion of the judgment.

E. Money judgment — basic order

  1. Immediate payment on demand.
  2. If not paid, levy on property of judgment obligor not exempt from execution.
  3. Garnishment of debts and credits in the hands of third persons where applicable.

F. Judgment for specific acts — Sec. 10

TypeExecution concept
Conveyance / delivery of deeds / specific actsIf party fails to comply within specified time, court may direct another person to perform at disobedient party’s cost; for property in the Philippines, court may divest and vest title by order.
Sale of real or personal propertyOfficer sells as directed by judgment and Rule 39.
Delivery or restitution of real propertyOfficer places entitled party in possession; removal/demolition follows rule-specific safeguards.
Delivery of personal propertyOfficer takes possession and delivers it to party entitled, with money component satisfied as provided.

G. Terceria / third-party claim — Sec. 16

A third person claiming property levied on may serve an affidavit of title/right to possession on the officer and a copy on the judgment obligee. The sheriff is not bound to keep the property if the judgment obligee fails to post the required indemnity bond on demand. This does not prevent a separate action to vindicate ownership/right.

Bond deadline
A claim for damages against the third-party-claim bond must be filed within the period specified by Rule 39; under the 2019 amendments, the relevant bond claim period is 120 days from filing of the bond.

H. Examination after judgment

Rule 39 provides post-judgment examination mechanisms to discover property or income of the judgment obligor and, in proper situations, obligations owed by third persons.

I. Effects of judgments

ConceptQuick formulation
Res judicata — bar by prior judgmentPrior final judgment bars relitigation of the same cause of action when requisites are present.
Conclusiveness of judgmentEven with a different cause of action, issues actually and directly adjudged may be conclusive between the same parties/privies.
Foreign judgmentsEffect depends on whether judgment is upon a specific thing or against a person; presumptive evidence may be repelled by want of jurisdiction, want of notice, collusion, fraud, or clear mistake of law or fact under Rule 39, Sec. 48.

10. Provisional Remedies — Rules 57–61

KWENTO: Main case is still pending, but waiting for final judgment may make the final victory useless. Provisional remedies preserve property, status quo, possession, or support while the principal case is unresolved.
RuleRemedyWhat it protects
57Preliminary AttachmentProperty / security for possible satisfaction; sometimes jurisdiction over the res
58Preliminary Injunction / TROStatus quo / prevention or compulsion regarding acts
59ReceivershipProperty or fund in danger of loss, waste, or dissipation
60ReplevinProvisional possession of personal property
61Support pendente liteTemporary support during pendency of action
Do not overgeneralize
Not every provisional remedy has the same affidavit, bond, notice, hearing, timing, or court requirements. Memorize each Rule separately.

11. Rule 57 — Preliminary Attachment

Purpose

  • Secure property for satisfaction of a future favorable judgment.
  • In appropriate cases, enable quasi in rem jurisdiction over property when personal jurisdiction over defendant is unavailable.

When

At the commencement of the action or at any time before entry of judgment, upon a ground expressly allowed by Rule 57.

Affidavit requirements — high yield

  • Sufficient cause of action exists.
  • Case falls under one of the grounds in Sec. 1.
  • No other sufficient security for the claim exists.
  • Amount due / value sought is at least the amount for which attachment is requested, over and above counterclaims.
Fraud trap
Nonpayment alone is not fraud. If attachment is based on fraudulent contracting of debt or fraudulent disposal of property, concrete facts showing fraudulent intent must be alleged, not merely the word “fraudulently.”

Attachment bond

The applicant posts a bond in the amount fixed by the court, conditioned to pay costs and damages sustained if attachment is later adjudged wrongful. Do not confuse this with replevin’s double-value bond.

Levy and summons

As a rule, levy must be preceded or accompanied by service of summons, complaint, application, affidavit, bond, and order, subject to the specific exceptions in Rule 57, Sec. 5 — including nonresident defendants and certain situations where personal/substituted service cannot be made despite diligent efforts.

12. Rule 58 — Preliminary Injunction & TRO

Preliminary injunction

An order at any stage before judgment requiring a party, court, agency, or person to refrain from a particular act; a mandatory injunction may require performance of an act.

Core requisites

  • Applicant has a clear and unmistakable right requiring protection.
  • There is a material and substantial invasion or threatened invasion.
  • Urgent necessity exists to prevent serious or irreparable injury.
  • No adequate ordinary remedy will sufficiently protect the right in time.

TRO time limits

IssuerPeriod
RTC / trial court ordinary TROUp to 20 days from service, within which injunction issue must be resolved.
Executive judge / presiding judge in extreme urgency72 hours ex parte from issuance; included in the maximum 20-day period.
Court of Appeals / member60 days from service.
Supreme Court / memberEffective until further orders.
Automatic expiration
The TRO cannot be extended or renewed on the same ground beyond the Rule’s maximum period. If preliminary injunction is not granted within the applicable period, the TRO is automatically vacated.

13. Rule 59 — Receivership

KWENTO: May property/fund in litigation at risk of being lost, removed, wasted, or materially injured. Instead of giving possession to either litigant, the court appoints a neutral receiver to preserve/administer it.

High-yield grounds

  • Applicant has an interest in property/fund that is in danger of being lost, removed, or materially injured.
  • In foreclosure, property is in danger of waste/dissipation/material injury and probably insufficient to discharge the mortgage debt, or conditions in the mortgage justify a receiver.
  • After judgment, to preserve property during appeal or to aid execution when appropriate.
  • Other cases when necessary to preserve property or rights of parties.

Appointment is extraordinary because it displaces possession/control; courts require a strong showing of necessity.

14. Rule 60 — Replevin

KWENTO: Personal property is allegedly wrongfully detained by defendant. Plaintiff wants provisional possession now, before final judgment.

Timing

Application may be made at the commencement of the action or at any time before the defendant answers.

Affidavit

  • Applicant is owner or entitled to possession.
  • Property is wrongfully detained by defendant, stating cause of detention according to best knowledge.
  • Actual market value.
  • Property has not been taken for tax/assessment/fine, seized under execution or attachment, or otherwise in custodia legis — or if so seized, it is exempt.

Bond

Applicant’s replevin bond = double the value of the property, conditioned for return if adjudged and payment of sums recovered against applicant.

Defendant may object to sufficiency of bond/sureties or seek return by posting a counterbond in accordance with the Rule.

15. Rule 61 — Support Pendente Lite

KWENTO: Habang pending ang action, may party or child who needs legally demandable support now, not years later after judgment.
  • Temporary support while the principal action is pending.
  • Application is supported by facts showing entitlement and financial conditions relevant to amount.
  • Court fixes provisional support after proper opportunity to respond/hearing under the Rule.
  • If final judgment shows recipient was not entitled to support, Rule 61 provides mechanisms concerning restitution.
Correction in approach
Do not memorize “support pendente lite is only RTC” as a universal abstract proposition. Jurisdiction depends on the principal action and governing statutes/rules; family courts are RTC branches for covered family matters. Focus first on where the principal action properly belongs.

Direct vs collateral attack

Direct attack: the action/proceeding is instituted specifically to annul, reverse, vacate, or set aside the judgment. Collateral attack: validity of judgment is challenged incidentally in another proceeding; generally disfavored except where nullity is apparent and the law allows it.

17. Verified Corrections & Lecture Traps

  1. Rule 39 ordinary writ motion: under the 2019 Rule 15, a motion for issuance of a writ of execution is non-litigious and not set for hearing. The older “motion without notice of hearing = scrap of paper” formulation is not the current general rule for this motion.
  2. Execution pending appeal: different from ordinary execution as a matter of right; it is litigious and requires good reasons in a special order.
  3. Rule 64 period: 30 days, not a fresh 15-day period after MR. MR interrupts the period; only the balance remains, with the Rule’s minimum protection.
  4. Attachment bond: do not use “double the value” — that belongs to replevin. Attachment bond is in the amount fixed by court under Rule 57.
  5. TRO: 72-hour ex parte TRO is not a separate extra period; it is included in the 20-day maximum for the trial-court TRO.
  6. Rule 34 and 35 attacks: the 2019 text expressly states that any court action on these motions is not subject to appeal or petitions for certiorari, prohibition, or mandamus.
  7. Demurrer denial: expressly not subject to appeal/certiorari/prohibition/mandamus before judgment under the amended Rule 33.
  8. Support pendente lite jurisdiction: avoid saying “only RTC” without tying it to the principal action and applicable jurisdictional law.
  9. Finality language: distinguish “final order for appeal” from “final and executory judgment.” They are not identical.

18. Recitation Drills

Q1. Plaintiff rests. Defendant says plaintiff’s evidence is insufficient. Remedy?
A: Demurrer to evidence under Rule 33.
Q2. Demurrer denied. Can defendant immediately file Rule 65 certiorari?
A: No. Under the amended Rule 33, denial is not subject to appeal or certiorari, prohibition, or mandamus before judgment.
Q3. Answer admits all material allegations. Best judgment-stage device?
A: Judgment on the pleadings under Rule 34, subject to exceptions such as specified matrimonial actions where material facts must still be proved.
Q4. Answer alleges payment, but Rule 26 admissions show no payment was made. Possible device?
A: Summary judgment under Rule 35 if no genuine issue of material fact remains.
Q5. RTC decided in original jurisdiction; mixed questions of fact and law. Mode?
A: Ordinary appeal under Rule 41 to the Court of Appeals.
Q6. RTC decided as appellate court. Mode to CA?
A: Petition for review under Rule 42.
Q7. Quasi-judicial agency decision; errors of fact and law. Mode?
A: Petition for review under Rule 43, if the agency is covered and no special rule provides otherwise.
Q8. COMELEC en banc final resolution allegedly issued with grave abuse. Mode?
A: Petition for certiorari under Rule 65 in relation to Rule 64, filed in the Supreme Court within the Rule 64 period.
Q9. Judgment final because lawyer simply forgot to appeal. Can Rule 38 automatically rescue client?
A: No. Rule 38 is extraordinary and requires FAME plus strict compliance with the double period and a meritorious cause/defense.
Q10. Rule 38 double period?
A: Within 60 days from knowledge and not more than 6 months from entry/taking of the judgment, final order, or proceeding.
Q11. Final RTC judgment, ordinary remedies unavailable without petitioner’s fault, ground is lack of jurisdiction. Remedy?
A: Annulment of judgment under Rule 47 in the Court of Appeals, subject to its requisites.
Q12. Final judgment entered 4 years ago. How enforce?
A: By motion under Rule 39, Sec. 6, assuming within five years from entry and no other bar.
Q13. Final judgment entered 7 years ago and not yet prescribed. How enforce?
A: By independent action to revive the judgment before prescription bars it.
Q14. Third person owns property wrongfully levied. Immediate Rule 39 device?
A: Third-party claim (terceria) under Sec. 16, without prejudice to a separate action.
Q15. Applicant says debtor “fraudulently refused to pay,” but alleges no concrete fraudulent acts. Attachment?
A: Insufficient if fraud is the relied-upon ground. Nonpayment alone does not establish fraud; specific facts showing fraudulent intent are required.
Q16. Replevin bond amount?
A: Double the value of the personal property.
Q17. Trial-court ex parte TRO in extreme urgency lasts 72 hours. Can another full 20 days be added?
A: No. The 72 hours are included in the total maximum 20-day period.
